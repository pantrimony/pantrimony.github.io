Pantrimony is a household app. It holds what your household eats, who is in it, and how to reach the person who cooks. This page says what that means.

Who is responsible

Pantrimony is run by Harit Mohan, an individual in Bengaluru, India. It is not a company. Questions, corrections and complaints go to support@pantrimony.com, and reach a person.

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, that makes him the data fiduciary for everything described here. If a complaint is not resolved, you can take it to the Data Protection Board of India.

What is collected, and why

This is the whole list.

Phone number
Identifies your account, and is how you sign in. Required.
Name
So the rest of your household knows who voted, and who bought the dal.
Email address
Optional. Used to send a sign-in link, and nothing else.
Password
Stored only as an Argon2 hash. The password itself is never written down. That is also why it cannot be recovered, only reset.
Household details
The household's name, its invite code, whether it eats veg, egg, non-veg or Jain, its timezone, meal times and voting window.
Who is in the household
Membership, role (admin or member), and when people joined or left. Anyone with the household's invite code sees its name, timezone and diet, and who is in it, with each person's role and the day they joined, before joining.
What is in the kitchen
Foods, quantities or levels, and a log of who changed what and when. So a disagreement about whether the rice ran out has an answer. When someone marks a meal cooked, or undoes that, the household sees who did it, and when. After a meal the app may ask what is running low. Once two or more people have answered, the household sees who answered and when, but it is never told what anyone said.
The shopping list
Items, their quantities, and any free text a member types to add one, with who added, bought or took each off, and when. So the dal is not bought twice.
Votes and preferences
Which dishes were suggested, how each member voted, and each member's food and dish preferences. Votes are visible to the household. That is the point of them. Everyone in the household can also see who set each ban, each favourite and each food preference.
Join attempts
Each attempt to use an invite code, with the outcome and time. Used only to stop codes being guessed, and deleted after 30 days.
Sign-in sessions
Hashed session tokens and their expiry. Never the token itself.
Your phone's notification details
The address Google's push service gives your phone, the language your notifications come in, and which kinds of notification you turned off. Kept until you sign out on that phone or delete your account; once the app is uninstalled, the next notification to that phone removes it.
Problem and crash reports
Only if the app crashes, or you send a report. A crash report holds what went wrong, which screen you were on, the app's build number, and a scrambled form of the network address it came from, used only to limit how many reports one address can send. A report you send also holds what you wrote. Phone numbers, email addresses and sign-in tokens are removed before either is stored. Deleted after 90 days.

What is not collected

  • No analytics, and no tracking. No Google Analytics, no third-party SDK watching what you tap.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No location.
  • No contacts, photos, or anything else from your phone beyond what you type in.
  • Nothing is sold, rented, or shared for anyone else's marketing.

Where it is stored

On a server in Mumbai, India, rented from Oracle Cloud. Your data does not leave India in the ordinary course of using the app. The database cannot be reached from the internet. It can only be read from the server itself, and getting into that server needs a key on a private network, not a password.

A backup of the database is taken every night, to storage in the same region, and kept for 30 days before it is deleted automatically. Backups exist so that a failure does not lose your household's history. They are also the one place where deleted data lingers for a while, and 30 days is the limit of that.

Who else sees it

Only services the app needs, and only what each one needs.

Resend
Sends sign-in emails. Receives an email address and the message.
Google
If you choose to sign in with Google, Google tells the app your name, email and account id. The app asks for nothing beyond that.
Google Firebase
Delivers push notifications: a nudge to vote, a question about what ran low after a meal, the day's cooking for a member who cooks, and alerts for admins. It receives your phone's push address, the text of each notification in your phone's language, and the ids the app needs to open the right screen. That text can name the household, a dish, the cook and a member of the household.
WhatsApp (Meta)
Not in use yet. Will carry the daily message to your cook. See below.

No one else. There is no data broker, no CRM, no marketing tool and no analytics vendor in this list, because none is used.

Your cook

The point of Pantrimony is to tell the person who cooks what to cook. Doing that means holding a phone number belonging to someone who does not use this app, and sending messages to it. So here is what the app does about that.

  • Only a household admin can add a cook's number, and only after confirming that they have the cook's agreement. The app records that confirmation, and the household's admins can see which admin gave it, and when.
  • If an admin sends the day's message to the cook themselves, the household sees which admin sent it, and when.
  • The number is used for one thing: sending that household's daily message. It is not used for anything else, and it does not leave the app's own systems except to send that message.
  • The messages say who is sending them, and how to stop receiving them.
  • Removing a cook from a household deletes the number.

If you are a cook, and you are getting these messages and would rather not, write to support@pantrimony.com and the number will be removed. You do not need an account to ask.

Children

Pantrimony is not meant for anyone under 18, and accounts must be created by adults. A household may of course cook for children. The app simply has no account for them, and none should be created on their behalf.

What you can ask for

A copy of your data
Write to support@pantrimony.com and you will get it.
A correction
Your name and email can be changed in the app. For anything else, ask.
Deletion
You can delete your account from inside the app, or ask for it by email without the app: Delete your account has the steps. What that does is described below, because it is not quite "everything vanishes".
To withdraw consent
Deleting your account withdraws it entirely. Leaving a household withdraws it for that household.

What deletion does

When you delete your account, your name becomes "Former member", your email is erased, your phone number is replaced with a value that cannot be dialled, your password hash is destroyed, and every session is revoked at once. The record that your phone was ever verified is erased too, because a note saying "this erased person had a verified number" is still information about you. Your dish and food preferences (your favourites, likes, bans, loves, dislikes and never-foods) are deleted too, in every household you were in.

What stays is the household's shared history: that someone bought rice on a Tuesday, and how a vote went. Those rows keep an anonymous identifier so the household's own records still make sense, and they show as "Former member". They cannot be traced back to you.

That includes your favourites on a vote that was already open. Your favourites are deleted, but a vote keeps, as part of how it went, which members favoured each option, and once your account is deleted you show there as "Former member".

This is a trade. Erasing the shared history would quietly rewrite other people's records of their own household. If you want that history removed as well, ask, and it will be discussed with the household.

How long things are kept

  • Your account, until you delete it.
  • Anonymised history, kept, as described above.
  • Consent records (that you agreed to this policy, and when) are kept for as long as the account or household they belong to is, as the record of what was agreed to.
  • Join attempts, 30 days.
  • Sign-in attempts, kept 30 days to stop a phone number or password being guessed. Only a scrambled form is kept, never the phone number, email or password itself.
  • Sign-in links, and password-reset and email-verification links, 7 days after they are created, whether or not they were used. Each one only ever works once, and stops working after 15-30 minutes; the 7 days is just how long the (by-then-useless) record is kept.
  • Push-notification device registrations, until you sign out that device or delete your account; once the app is uninstalled, the next notification to that phone removes it.
  • The household's message history (cook briefs, vote nudges and the like), kept as part of the household's record, the same as its votes and meal history.
  • The shopping list, each item until it is bought or taken off and for 30 days after that, then deleted automatically; whoever added or bought it shows as "Former member" once they delete their account.
  • Your dish and food preferences (favourites, likes, bans, loves, dislikes and never-foods), until you change them or delete your account. In a household you leave, or are removed from, they stop counting, and they count again if you rejoin.
  • Backups, 30 days, then deleted automatically.
  • Sessions, until they expire, which is at most 30 days. Signing out stops a session working at once, but its record is kept, unusable, until that date. That way a stolen copy used later is spotted, and every session is ended.

Security

Passwords are hashed with Argon2. Session tokens are stored hashed. Traffic is encrypted in transit. The database is not exposed to the internet, and administrative access needs a key on a private network.

It is worth adding that this is a small app run by one person, not a company with a security team. It is built carefully, but nobody should give it information whose exposure would be serious. It holds what you eat, not your money.

If this page changes

Material changes will be announced in the app before they take effect, and the app records which version of this policy you agreed to. This page always shows the current one.
